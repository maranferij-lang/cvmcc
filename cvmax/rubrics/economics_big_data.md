# Program rubric: Economics and Big Data (KSE)

Typical targets: data analyst, business/financial analyst, research assistant or predoc, junior
economist, risk analyst in banks, product analyst.

What recruiters look for:
- Quantitative coursework named: econometrics, statistics, mathematics, programming, finance
  (Federal Reserve Board RA programme).
- Programming languages named and used: R, Stata, Python, SQL; LaTeX for research roles (Yale, Penn/NBER
  predoc postings).
- Data-handling evidence: cleaning and merging data, codebooks, tables and figures, literature reviews.
- Every data bullet names the tool, the dataset size and the method, and says what was found.
- At least one project with a link to code or output (GitHub, report).
- Research experience and thesis with title and advisor for RA/predoc roles.

Common mistakes:
- A bare tool list ("Excel, SPSS, Stata, R, Python, Power BI") with no project showing any of them.
- A regression described without the question, the data or the finding; over-claiming causality.
- No scale (observations, sources, weeks). GPA without a scale.
- An academic CV sent to an industry analyst role.

Format: industry analyst: Education (GPA with scale, coursework, thesis) -> Experience -> Projects (links)
-> Skills grouped (Programming, Tools, Languages). One page. RA/predoc: Education -> Research Experience
-> Papers/Presentations -> Work -> Skills; two pages acceptable in UK/European academia.

Example bullets (style reference only):
- Cleaned and merged 12 years of regional labour-force survey data (~180,000 observations) in Stata;
  built a codebook used by 3 faculty co-authors.
- Estimated a difference-in-differences model of a minimum-wage increase on regional employment in R;
  presented results at a 25-person research seminar.
- Wrote SQL queries on a 4M-row transactions table to segment 60,000 customers by churn risk; findings
  informed a retention campaign.
- Automated a monthly CPI dashboard in Python (pandas, Plotly), cutting preparation from 2 days to 3 hours.
