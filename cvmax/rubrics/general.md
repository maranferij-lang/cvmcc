# General rubric for student and early-career CVs (international market, English)

## Criteria (score each 1-5)
1. **Target fit**: the CV reads as written for this role and company type. Most relevant experience and
   skills come first. Irrelevant items are cut or shortened.
2. **Impact bullets**: bullets describe results, not duties. Pattern: strong action verb + what you did +
   how + measurable result. "Responsible for...", "Helped with...", "Participated in..." are weak.
3. **Evidence and numbers**: claims are backed by numbers, scope, tools, links (GitHub, portfolio,
   publications). Skills listed in the skills section also appear in experience or projects.
4. **Structure and scannability**: a recruiter finds name, contacts, education, most relevant
   experience and key skills in 6-10 seconds. Reverse chronological order. Consistent dates and format.
5. **Length and density**: one page for students and graduates with under ~5 years of experience.
   No walls of text. 2-5 bullets per role, 1-2 lines per bullet.
6. **ATS-friendliness**: plain single-column layout is safest. No text inside images, no key info in
   headers or footers, standard section names, keywords from the vacancy used naturally.
7. **Language quality**: correct, concise English. No first-person pronouns. Consistent tense
   (past for past roles, present for current).

## Section order for students
Header (name, city/country, email, phone, LinkedIn, GitHub/portfolio if relevant) ->
Education (university, program, expected graduation, GPA if strong, relevant coursework, exchange,
scholarships) -> Experience -> Projects -> Leadership & Activities -> Skills -> Languages
(with level and certificates, e.g. "English: C1 (IELTS 7.5)") -> optional Awards.
Move Projects above Experience when projects are more relevant than jobs.

## International conventions to enforce
- No photo, date of birth, age, marital status, nationality or full home address for EU/US/international
  applications. City and country are enough.
- No "Objective" paragraph full of generic adjectives. A 1-2 line summary is fine only if it is specific.
- No soft-skill lists without evidence ("hardworking, team player, stress-resistant"). Show them in bullets.
- Consistent date format (e.g. "Sep 2024 - Present").
- Language levels on the CEFR scale or with a test score.
- Email should look professional.
- School (high school) achievements only if exceptional (olympiad winner) and the candidate is in years 1-2.

## Things students usually undersell (ask about them in Grill me)
Case competitions and hackathons (with placement), student organisations and roles in them,
volunteering, coursework or thesis projects with real data, teaching assistant work, tutoring,
freelance, research assistance, online courses with a finished project, olympiads, scholarships,
exchange semesters, organising events (numbers of participants, budget).

## Role disambiguation
The same title means different work in different places. Examples:
- Business Analyst in consulting: client problem solving, Excel/PowerPoint modelling, structured
  communication, market research. In fintech or product IT: requirements, user stories, SQL, BPMN,
  working with developers and product managers.
- Data Analyst in a bank: SQL, reporting, risk data, regulatory accuracy. In a product company: SQL,
  A/B tests, product metrics, dashboards, Python.
- Project Manager in IT outsourcing: delivery, Jira, Scrum, client communication. In NGO or public sector:
  grants, donors, reporting, stakeholder coordination.
Always tie advice to the company type and the vacancy text. When neither is given, state your assumption.

## Gaps advice
Recommend what moves the needle for this exact target: specific tools, a portfolio project with a clear
deliverable, a language certificate with a target score, a relevant certification, a case competition.
Prefer free or cheap options, and things doable during the semester. Order by impact per week of effort.
