# Program rubric: Law

Typical targets: law firm intern, in-house legal in tech or banks, compliance, policy analyst,
international organisations, legal tech.

What recruiters look for:
- Research, writing and analysis made visible (Yale Law, UChicago): memos, briefs, publications.
- Legal-adjacent activities: legal clinics (cases, type), moot courts (role and result), law journals,
  research for professors.
- Areas of law and jurisdictions named precisely (Ukrainian civil law, EU law, sanctions, GDPR,
  AML/KYC, arbitration, IHL).
- Grades and modules in detail for UK firms; dissertation title (ULaw).
- Motivation for law shown through choices and activities (Freshfields via TARGETjobs).
- Attention to detail: zero typos, perfectly consistent formatting (around 80% fail at the paper stage).
- Scrupulous honesty; language levels that can be demonstrated (ABA).

Common mistakes: creative or coloured formats (black text only, Michigan Law); a "Relevant work
experience" heading (split into legal and non-legal instead); inflating how law-related a job was
(Berkeley Law); moot courts without role or result; no jurisdiction; confidential client or matter names.

Format: US-style firms: one page, Education (honors, journals, moot court) -> Experience -> Languages ->
Interests. UK firms: up to two sides of A4, name as title, city only, Education -> Legal Experience ->
Non-legal Experience -> Skills -> Extracurriculars. In-house/compliance/policy: one-page business format
with regulatory frameworks in the bullets and links to writing samples.

Example bullets (style reference only):
- Drafted 9 research memoranda on Ukrainian and EU sanctions regimes for a law firm's trade practice;
  2 were adapted into client alerts.
- Represented the university as oralist in the Jessup moot court (national round); team ranked 3rd of 14.
- Reviewed 60+ supply contracts for GDPR compliance; flagged 11 non-compliant data-transfer clauses.
- Advised 35 displaced clients on compensation procedures at a legal clinic, under attorney supervision.
