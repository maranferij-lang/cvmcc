# Program rubric: Psychology (KSE)

Typical targets: HR and people analytics, UX research, research assistant, NGO psychosocial programmes,
L&D, customer research.

What recruiters look for:
- For UX: an industry resume, not an academic CV (NN/g). Portfolio link in the header.
- Research methods named and evidenced: interviews, usability tests, surveys, experiments, thematic
  analysis; sample sizes; tools (SPSS, R, JASP, Qualtrics, NVivo, Python).
- Bullets shaped as verb + quantitative outcome + context + tool (NN/g).
- GPA listed; coursework described in plain terms (2-3 lines), without academic jargon.
- Lab research with role, lab, university, PI and dates; posters and publications in APA with the
  candidate's name bolded.
- For NGO/UN roles: the vacancy's competency language and Context-Action-Result bullets (Tufts);
  trainings such as Psychological First Aid and the supervision context.
- For HR analytics: Excel, Power BI, survey analysis, stakeholder communication.
- Any real work experience helps, even unrelated.

Common mistakes: long skill lists; headshots; objective statements; references; duty-only research
bullets ("Coded participant answers"); breaching confidentiality (client details); clinical claims
("provided therapy") beyond the student's actual scope and supervision.

Format: Education -> Experience/Internships -> Research -> Leadership -> Skills (Research methods;
Analysis tools; Languages). One page for industry roles.

Example bullets (style reference only):
- Moderated 14 remote usability sessions for a banking app's onboarding; synthesised 5 friction points,
  3 of which were fixed in the next release.
- Surveyed 310 students with the System Usability Scale; mean SUS rose from 58 to 71 after redesign.
- Coded 42 interview transcripts in NVivo with a second coder (Cohen's kappa 0.82).
- Co-facilitated 12 group support sessions for displaced teenagers under a licensed psychologist's
  supervision.
